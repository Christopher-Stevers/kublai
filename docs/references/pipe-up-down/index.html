Pipe up / down references

Five verified crops from two publicly published drawing legends. Saved October 19, 2026. Original labels are retained. This collection supports future generic recognition; it has not changed the app’s detector.

What to look for

  • Use the reviewed legend for the current drawing set as the authority. These are reference examples, not universal symbol definitions or a deployed detector.
  • Prioritize endpoints of the known pipe for turned elbows. Search along the run too: vertical tee outlets and rise/drop transitions can occur where the plan line continues.
  • In these two legends, single-line up elbows have a closed round end; down elbows have an interrupted round end. The connection to the pipe and the gap orientation matter. Do not infer direction from a circle alone.
  • Grady also uses internal curved detail and both single-line and double-line depictions. Preserve those differences when matching rather than reducing every symbol to a generic circle.
  • Normalize rotation and scale using the local pipe direction; keep the exact source geometry and open/closed topology. Adjacent symbols must remain separate.
  • A simple endpoint is not sufficient evidence: caps, disconnected geometry, cropped lines, fixture connections and line breaks can look similar.
  • UP/DN text can describe pipe pitch. Flow arrows are not necessarily rise/drop symbols. Grady labels one transition as drop OR rise, so its shape alone does not resolve direction.
  • Locate the matched shape on the pipe; nearby text may identify it but must not become the placement coordinate. Keep unresolved direction or identity as review candidates.
  • All five PNGs were visually checked against their original PDF graphics and labels. These are legend examples; no in-plan occurrence has been labeled as verified ground truth in this reference collection.
